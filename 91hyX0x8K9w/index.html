Dindigul Chicken Biriyani

One-pot basmati version · interactive cook-along

Chicken quantity:

Ingredients — 1 kg chicken

  • Chicken — 1 kg
    ~140 g pieces; keep leg pieces aside
  • Basmati rice — 1 kg
    rinse & soak 20–30 min before starting
  • Water — 1 : 1.5
    rice-to-water by volume, measured in the same vessel
  • Groundnut oil + ghee
    a few tbsp of each; the combo is the point
  • Star anise — 2
    whole, for tempering + paste
  • Cinnamon sticks — 3–4 in
    generous — signature of Dindigul
  • Green cardamom — 8–10
    generous — signature of Dindigul
  • Cloves — 8–10
    generous — signature of Dindigul
  • Onions — 2 large
    sliced thin & elongated
  • Mutton fat — ≈50 g
    small piece; keeps rice moist & fragmented
  • Green chillies — 8–10
    small round; use more if long thin ones
  • Garlic cloves — ≈30 g
    sliced, for aroma
  • Mint leaves
    a generous handful — mandatory
  • Coriander leaves
    a handful, finely chopped
  • Ginger-garlic paste — 135 g garlic + 65 g ginger
    garlic must exceed ginger (2:1)
  • Curd — 65–70 g
    only if the masala starts sticking
  • Lemon — 1 small
    juice added after water — never sautéed
  • Salt
    to taste; fix seasoning before dum

Method — step by step

  1. Soak the rice, blend the spice paste. Rinse and soak the basmati 20–30 min. Blend the star anise, cinnamon, cardamom and cloves dry first (cardamom stays coarse), then with a splash of water to a fine paste. Timer: 20 min
  2. Temper the whole spices. Heat groundnut oil + ghee together; temper the whole spices. No bay leaves — the Dindigul way.
  3. Build the base. Sauté the thin-sliced onions; add the mutton fat after they soften (adding it to screaming-hot oil makes it burst). Add green chillies, sliced garlic and mint leaves; cook till everything caramelises.
  4. Cook the masala. Add ginger-garlic paste → spice paste → coriander leaves. Cook till oil releases and it turns dark brown. Stir in the curd if it sticks; a splash of water if it gets too thick. Timer: 10 min
  5. Chicken in — big pieces first. Add the big chicken pieces (not the legs); cook 10–12 min to about 80% done — not fully, the rice phase finishes them. Salt now so the chicken takes the seasoning. Timer: 11 min
  6. Leg pieces, water, lemon. Add the leg pieces (late, so they stay whole). Pour water at 1 : 1.5 rice-to-water. Add the lemon juice now — adding it earlier turns it bitter.
  7. Rice goes in. Bring to a full rolling boil — mandatory, it kills any raw smell. Strain the soaked rice and add it to the boiling mixture.
  8. High flame — watch the water. High flame only until the water is just absorbed and the rice is ~70% done. Stop earlier than the video's 90% — basmati finishes on steam. Timer: 8 min
  9. Dum. Turn to low flame, seal, and leave undisturbed 10–15 min. Timer: 12 min
  10. Rest & serve. Rest 10 min off the heat, then open. Rice should be fragmented and moist, leg pieces whole. Serve piping hot with dalcha. Timer: 10 min

Seeraga samba is the authentic grain (1:2 water, cook to 90% dry, 20-min dum). This page is the basmati one-pot adaptation: 1:1.5 water, rice in at ~70% done, 10–15-min dum. Spice amounts the video calls “generous” are sensible scaled estimates.
